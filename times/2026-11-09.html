Nvidia Opens Investigation Into CEO Jensen Huang

The board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised, as Congress asks for answers

Nvidia's board said on Sunday it is opening an independent investigation after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. A committee of independent directors, advised by outside counsel, will oversee the inquiry.

The New York Times and the Financial Times reported on Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman who is not related to him. Her Singapore-based company, Megaspeed, was buying Nvidia's advanced chips, and U.S. intelligence reportedly concluded she had been recruited by China's top intelligence agency. The New York Times put Megaspeed's purchases at about $4.8 billion in equipment. The Chinese Embassy has denied any spying allegations.

In an email to staff, Huang acknowledged an extramarital relationship and apologised to his wife, children and colleagues. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and a spokesperson said Huang remains CEO.

The politics are heating up. House Republicans led by China Select Committee chairman John Moolenaar have asked Huang to testify on November 18, and Senator Elizabeth Warren called for bipartisan legislation on advanced chip exports to China. President Trump called Huang a fine person and an honorable guy.

The timing is awkward for the company. Nvidia is worth nearly $4.5 trillion, and it fell 9.2% on November 5 during a tech selloff in which the Nasdaq 100 lost 6.3% and the semiconductor index dropped 12.3%.
