Anthropic sets aside 5% of its shares for a public benefit fund

The new fund, with a further 2% for every 50% rise in value, brings roughly one-fifth of the company's shares dedicated to the public good.

Anthropic announced on Sunday that it will commit 5% of its shares to a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval. The company says the fund gives it more room to pursue its mission beyond the activities it runs itself.

The commitment can grow. Under the plan, the board may add 2% for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline. Anthropic said it will share more about the fund's structure, governance and valuation milestones in the coming months and will publish a public benefit report on its progress.

The fund is set up as a wholly-owned LLC rather than a 501(c)(3). Anthropic said none of its equity in the fund will be used for lobbying or political giving, in the US or abroad. Examples of what it might do include large donations to traditional nonprofits and direct distributions to specific populations.

Labor is a named focus. Anthropic has already committed $200 million to studying AI's effects on workers, and said the fund may support bolder approaches to the issue. The company also noted that its co-founders pledged 80% of their equity at founding, and that about half of employees take part in an equity donation match program.

Counting those earlier pledges, Anthropic says roughly 20% of its shares will be dedicated to the public good, plus any extra percentage from the valuation milestones. The announcement came days after Anthropic said it had given the nonprofit evaluator METR employee-like access to its systems, with METR free to publish its own risk assessments.

Türkiye

Türkiye's new constitution must go to a referendum, adviser says

Presidential chief adviser Mehmet Ucum said Türkiye's new constitution process began on October 1 with Erdogan's speech to parliament, and that voters must approve any new charter even if every lawmaker backs it.

He suggested the parliament seated after the 2028 elections could adopt a text in 2029 and put it to a national vote. He said the drafts he reviewed keep the two-term presidential limit, while parties remain divided over presidential, parliamentary and semi-presidential systems.

AI safety

Anthropic gives METR employee-like access to its systems

Anthropic has given a team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models, employee-like access to its systems, and METR will publish its own risk assessments. SecureBio and Accenture are also evaluators, and talks are under way with others, including Hugging Face.

Anthropic can require redaction of sensitive categories such as customer data and model architectures, but cannot veto what METR publishes.
