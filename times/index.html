AI · Open standards

Anthropic gives away Agent Skills, the standard behind a million agent playbooks

The format for teaching AI agents specialist know-how moves to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, with more than 70 platforms already on board.

Anthropic said on 22 October that it is donating the Agent Skills open standard to the Agentic AI Foundation, a body under the Linux Foundation. A skill is simply a folder with a SKILL.md file plus any scripts or reference material an agent needs to do a specialised job.

Along with the specification, Anthropic is handing over the Agent Skills name and logo, its GitHub organization and the agentskills.io domain. The foundation was co-founded last year by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI, and Anthropic previously donated the Model Context Protocol to it as well.

The company says adoption is already broad. More than 70 agent platforms have integrated skills since the standard was published in December 2025, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code. About 1 million skills have been published and nearly 280 million installed.

Anthropic explains that skills grew out of work on Claude Code: Claude handled general knowledge work well but often lacked the procedural knowledge that real business tasks need. After the October 2025 launch, developers wrote thousands of skills and loaders for other agents, which led Anthropic to publish the standard openly.

Engineers from Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and other companies will maintain the standard as individuals, with David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag as lead maintainers. The proposed roadmap covers versioned releases, a formal proposal process and security guidance for people who write and install skills. Anthropic says the aim is to keep the standard open, community-driven and vendor-neutral.

What Utku should do

  1. Write one useful skill as a SKILL.md folder, test it in two different agents, and publish it on GitHub. That shows the portability the standard promises.
  2. Watch the foundation's roadmap on security guidance and consider contributing a review of how skills with scripts could be abused. Early contributors to a new standard become known quickly.

AI · Models

Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro lands close to Claude Opus 5.5

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro. It scored 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 58.2% and just behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4%, according to OfficeChai.

It costs $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, and Moonshot has promised open weights by 4 November.
